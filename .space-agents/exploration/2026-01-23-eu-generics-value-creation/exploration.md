# European Generics+OTC Value Creation Strategy

**Date:** 2026-01-23
**Context:** Post-acquisition 100-day planning (CapVest acquiring from Bain/Cinven)
**Asset Profile:** European generics + OTC/consumer health company
**Audience:** C-suite executives, Board, Investors
**Geographic Focus:** EU-wide

---

## Executive Summary

This exploration identifies the top 5 challenges facing European generics+OTC companies through 2030, and evaluates 10 high-impact value creation bets. Analysis framed around two primary levers: **DTC marketing** and **volume growth**.

**Key Finding:** Pursue sustainability-first (regulatory deadlines), then layer digital health and channel strategies.

---

## Part 1: Top 5 Challenges (2025-2030)

### 1. E-Pharmacy Channel Disruption
- Market: USD 29.6B (2023) → USD 79.4B (2029), **17.85% CAGR**
- dm entering Dec 2025, targeting 9% German market by 2030
- Rossmann/Lidl entering 2027; Amazon anticipated
- Redcare Pharmacy: EUR 2.9B, nearly doubled Rx sales in Germany 2025

### 2. Private Label Cannibalization
- 7 of 15 top e-pharmacies have own brands
- Private labels: **41% of top-3 search rankings**
- Private label OTC: **9.78% CAGR** (vs 4% overall market)
- 53% of European consumers choose private labels predominantly

### 3. DTC Marketing Restrictions
- UK HFSS ban **Jan 5, 2026** (9pm watershed + online ad ban)
- Germany OLG Cologne (Sept 2025): Instagram Reels = TV ads
- EU Pharma Package reform by 2028
- Celebrity/influencer endorsements prohibited for OTC

### 4. Tender Concentration Risk
- **83% of critical generics** have single dominant supplier
- Price remains lead criterion in most markets
- Nordic Pharmaceutical Forum: multi-country coordination emerging

### 5. Sustainability Disclosure
- NHS: **10% minimum sustainability weighting** since 2022
- By 2030: must demonstrate emissions progress for NHS eligibility
- CSRD still applies to large companies despite delays

### B. Portfolio & Geography (Agent 2 - COMPLETE)

#### Bet 4: Biosimilars Expansion
**Market:** EUR 11.4B (2025) growing to EUR 38-65B by 2034-35, **17-18% CAGR**
- 119 EMA-approved biosimilars (mid-2025); record 28 approvals in 2024, 21 in H1 2025
- Europe holds **37% global biosimilar market share** - largest regional market
- Monoclonal antibodies represent 47% of segment; oncology largest therapeutic area
- Key upcoming patent cliffs: Keytruda (pembrolizumab), Eylea (aflibercept), Prolia/Xgeva (denosumab)

**Approach:** Partner for commercialization rights (STADA/Alvotech model) over in-house development
- Biosimilar development: EUR 100-250M per molecule vs EUR 1-2B for originator biologics
- Time-to-market: 7-8 years development cycle; requires Phase 3 comparability studies
- Partner with established biosimilar developers (Alvotech, Celltrion, Samsung Bioepis, Bio-Thera)

**Case Studies:**
- **STADA/Alvotech partnership:** 7 marketed biosimilars in Europe; Uzpruvo (ustekinumab) first-to-market July 2024, targeting EUR 2.5B EU market; Hukyndra (adalimumab) capturing share in EUR 4B+ segment
- **Celltrion Steqeyma:** Launched in 5 major EU markets (Jan 2025) within 6 months of approval
- **Sandoz spin-off:** World's largest biosimilars business; EUR 1.6B revenue, 8 marketed molecules

**Risks:**
- Clustering of launches compresses pricing (5 ustekinumab biosimilars approved within 12 months)
- Manufacturing complexity requires specialized facilities (mammalian cell culture)
- Patent litigation risk (J&J settlements required for ustekinumab market entry)
- 2026 performance-based pricing for DiGA biosimilar-adjacent products

**100-Day Actions:**
- Days 1-30: Map biosimilar pipeline opportunities (patent cliffs 2026-2030); identify 3-5 partnership targets
- Days 31-60: Initiate commercial partnership discussions; due diligence on manufacturing capabilities
- Days 61-100: Sign LOI with preferred partner; establish European commercialization infrastructure

**Capital:** EUR 50-150M for exclusive commercialization rights; EUR 200-400M for co-development partnership

#### Bet 5: CEE Geographic Expansion
**Market:** CEE pharmaceutical market growing at **4-5% CAGR** (vs 2-3% Western Europe)
- Poland: EUR 12B market, 88% generics penetration by volume, 66% by value - **fastest growing**
- Hungary, Czech Republic: Mandatory generic substitution policies implemented
- Eastern Europe pharma spending: USD 13.7B projected by 2030
- CEE becoming hub for clinical trials, regulatory outsourcing, pharmacovigilance

**Approach:** Replicate Krka's vertically integrated model from regional base
- Leverage lower manufacturing costs (30-40% below Western Europe)
- Build local regulatory/commercial presence before expanding West
- Target branded generics segment (8.3% CAGR) - higher margins than commodity generics

**Case Studies:**
- **Krka (Slovenia):** EUR 2.0B revenue (2024), EUR 2.13B guidance for 2026; 70+ countries; 13,344 employees; net profit EUR 383M (2025 est.); 5-year revenue CAGR 6.3%; vertically integrated from API to finished dosage; 14 EPO patent applications (2024) - #1 among Slovenian companies
- **STADA "Local Heroes":** Acquired regional brands across CEE; leveraged local brand equity with pan-European distribution
- **Zentiva (Advent):** Built CEE platform through acquisitions; sold to Sanofi, then spun out; now EUR 1.5B+ revenue

**Risks:**
- Fragmented regulatory landscape (EMA centralized vs national procedures)
- Currency volatility (non-Euro markets: Poland PLN, Hungary HUF, Czech CZK)
- Pricing pressure from government cost-containment measures
- Talent competition from Western European pharma subsidiaries

**100-Day Actions:**
- Days 1-30: Market sizing and competitive mapping for Poland, Czech Republic, Hungary; identify acquisition targets
- Days 31-60: Regulatory pathway assessment; establish local advisory board
- Days 61-100: Initiate discussions with 2-3 regional targets; begin registration dossier transfers

**Capital:** EUR 100-300M for platform acquisition; EUR 20-50M for greenfield market entry

#### Bet 6: M&A Roll-Up
**Market:** European pharma M&A reached USD 746B (2025), +12% YoY; H2 2025 up 23% vs H1
- 50% of dealmakers expect activity increase in 2026
- Pharma/biotech among most active sectors alongside TMT and energy
- PE roll-up strategies under increased scrutiny (serial acquisitions assessed holistically)

**Approach:** Disciplined bolt-on acquisitions with clear regulatory pathway
- Target EUR 50-200M revenue companies with complementary portfolios
- Focus on branded OTC, specialty generics, niche therapeutic areas
- Avoid horizontal overlaps requiring divestitures; prefer vertical integration

**Case Studies:**
- **Cooper/Viatris (2024):** EUR 1.2B combined revenue post-acquisition; EC Phase 1 approval with conditions - required divestiture of Bebegel (Portugal) and Oxtowaxol (Germany) due to high combined market shares; approved purchasers Uriach and InfectoPharm
- **Atida (buy-and-build):** E-pharmacy roll-up across multiple markets; successful PE exit 2024
- **STADA (Bain/Cinven):** EUR 4B+ acquisition (2017); built through 50+ acquisitions across Europe

**EC Regulatory Considerations:**
- Article 22 referrals: Below-threshold deals can be called in (post-Illumina/GRAIL limitations)
- "Killer acquisition" scrutiny in pharma - DG Comp study (Nov 2024) on pipeline discontinuations
- 15+ Member States introducing/expanding call-in powers in 2025
- New EU Merger Guidelines expected Q4 2027 with "innovation shield" for small targets

**Risks:**
- Phase 2 investigation risk if horizontal overlaps identified (average 4-6 month delay)
- Divestiture requirements can reduce deal value by 10-20%
- Integration complexity across multiple regulatory jurisdictions
- Financing headwinds - dealmakers cite financing as top challenge

**100-Day Actions:**
- Days 1-30: Build acquisition target pipeline (10-15 companies); engage M&A advisors
- Days 31-60: Preliminary competition assessment on shortlisted targets; financing structure
- Days 61-100: Initiate discussions with 2-3 preferred targets; prepare EC pre-notification

**Capital:** EUR 200-500M for platform deals; EUR 50-100M for bolt-on acquisitions

### C. Operations & Supply (Agent 3 - COMPLETE)

#### Bet 7: API Reshoring
**Market Context:** India/China produce **60-80% of APIs** consumed in Europe
- European API market: USD 21.7B (2024)
- Cost disadvantage: Asian APIs **35-40% cheaper** than European production
- China controls ~80% of global generic API supply chain
- 200 critical medicines identified by EMA; 83% have single dominant supplier

**EU Policy Drivers:**
- **IPCEI Med4Cure:** EUR 1B total public funding; 13 companies across 6 Member States
- **IPCEI Tech4Cure (July 2025):** EUR 403M public funding + EUR 826M private investment
- **Critical Medicines Act:** Expected 2026 - coordinated procurement, environmental standards parity
- France 2030: EUR 140M to EUROAPI for corticosteroids, nanocrystals, macrolide antibiotics

**Approach:** Selective reshoring of critical/high-value APIs with EU funding support
- Target 50-70% cost gap closure through automation (Industry 4.0) and green manufacturing
- Focus on shortage-prone molecules: antibiotics, corticosteroids, essential generics
- Leverage IPCEI framework for up to 50% co-funding on eligible R&D activities

**Case Studies:**
- **EUROAPI Med4Cure (Oct 2025):** 3 priority areas - corticosteroids (Vertolaye), nanocrystals, macrolide antibiotics (Saint-Aubin-lès-Elbeuf); partnership ecosystem with startups, SMEs, academia
- **Krka vertical integration:** In-house API production as strategic competitive advantage; EUR 140M CAPEX planned for 2026; new joint venture in India for cost-competitive sourcing
- **Aschimfarma proposal:** EUR 1.5B investment call for new EU capacity in critical APIs at risk of shortages

**Cost Comparison (API Manufacturing):**
| Cost Factor | EU (Baseline) | India | China |
|-------------|---------------|-------|-------|
| Labor Index | 100 | ~10 | ~8 |
| Overall Savings | 0% | 30-35% | 35-40% |
| Electricity | Higher | ~19c/kWh | ~11c/kWh |

**Risks:**
- Persistent cost gap even with subsidies (automation can close 50-60% at best)
- Long lead times for facility construction (3-5 years from approval to production)
- EUROAPI restructuring - closed Brindisi (Italy), Haverhill (UK) facilities
- Chinese firms gaining market share despite reshoring initiatives

**100-Day Actions:**
- Days 1-30: Audit current API supply chain; identify critical dependencies and shortage-prone molecules
- Days 31-60: Assess IPCEI eligibility; engage with Member State funding bodies
- Days 61-100: Develop business case for 2-3 priority API investments; submit pre-application to national IPCEI coordinators

**Capital:** EUR 50-150M over 3-5 years (assuming 50% IPCEI co-funding on EUR 100-300M total project)

#### Bet 8: Tender Optimization
**Market Context:** Public procurement dominates European hospital pharma markets
- Price remains primary criterion in most markets; sustainability criteria emerging
- **83% of critical generics** have single dominant supplier - concentration risk
- Nordic Pharmaceutical Forum: 10-year anniversary (Sept 2025); most integrated pharma region in EU

**Nordic Model - Best Practice:**
- **Joint tendering:** Denmark, Norway, Sweden, Finland, Iceland; Region Skane (Sweden) joining 2026
- **Environmental criteria:** Applied in joint Nordic tenders since 2021; 40% of EU tenders by 2026, 75% by 2030 (Amgros target)
- **Norway precedent:** 15% price premium accepted for lower-carbon supplier; 21% carbon footprint reduction achieved
- **Knowledge sharing:** Horizon scanning, ATMP guidelines, security of supply coordination

**Sustainability Criteria in Tenders:**
- NHS England: 10% minimum sustainability weighting since 2022
- Amgros (Denmark): Suppliers won contracts on environmental criteria over price
- MEAT criteria (Most Economically Advantageous Tender): Quality, safety, environmental sustainability

**Approach:** Build tender excellence capability with sustainability differentiation
- Establish dedicated tender management function with market intelligence
- Develop sustainability credentials aligned with Nordic/NHS criteria
- Explore participation in multi-country tender frameworks

**Case Studies:**
- **Sykehusinnkjøp HF (Norway):** Environmental criteria integrated; legal challenge by competitor rejected; sustainability criteria validated
- **Amgros/Nordic Pharmaceutical Forum:** Joint price negotiations; common environmental criteria template; included in EC Critical Medicines Alliance
- **NHS Evergreen:** Supplier registration requires emissions data; by 2030 must demonstrate progress

**Risks:**
- Fragmented criteria across markets - no harmonized EU standard yet
- Capacity constraints if winning large multi-country tenders
- Price erosion from tender aggregation (counter: sustainability premium)
- Compliance cost for smaller suppliers creates barrier

**100-Day Actions:**
- Days 1-30: Audit current tender participation rates and win rates by market; identify gaps
- Days 31-60: Establish tender intelligence function; map upcoming tenders across priority markets
- Days 61-100: Develop sustainability response template; register for NHS Evergreen; explore Nordic Pharmaceutical Forum observer status

**Capital:** EUR 5-15M for tender management capability and systems; EUR 10-20M for sustainability compliance investments
